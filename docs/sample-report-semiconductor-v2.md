## 1. Executive Summary

European semiconductor manufacturing is concentrating in a single corridor: Saxony/Dresden, Germany. With over €17B in committed investment across four major fabs — ESMC/TSMC (€10B+), Infineon Smart Power Fab (€5B, opened July 2026), GlobalFoundries SPRINT (€1.1B), and Bosch (1B) — Dresden is now Europe's largest semiconductor manufacturing cluster [1, 3]. Secondary clusters exist in the Czech Republic (onsemi SiC, €1.64B), the Netherlands (NXP/ASML ecosystem), Ireland (Intel), and France (STMicro Agrate, €1B), but none approach the scale or density of the Saxony corridor.

Commercial real estate markets across Europe are in a phase of structural consolidation. Prime logistics rents grew +2.7% YoY in Q1 2026 across 45 European markets (BNP Paribas), but growth is decelerating from the 2020–2023 inflation-driven peak. The EU27 commercial property price index stands at 114.1 (Q4 2025, ECB RESC), up 13.7% from the 2015 base but flat since Q1 2024 — indicating a market that has stabilised after post-pandemic repricing.

**Primary recommendation:** Establish the finishing/warehouse facility in the **Saxony/Dresden corridor (NUTS-2: DE80)**. Proximity to €17B+ in customer manufacturing, same-day logistics to all four major fabs, established transport infrastructure, and access to Silicon Saxony's 130+ company ecosystem make this the optimal location. Prime logistics rents in German Big Five hubs sit at approximately €60–120/sqm/year (IndiFind/Savills), with prime yields stabilising at ~4.5% (Savills 2026).

**Alternative recommendation:** **Wrocław/Lower Silesia (NUTS-2: PL51)** as a lower-cost satellite — 40–50% lower industrial rents (€30–60/sqm/year vs. Germany's €60–120), rail-linked to Dresden (2.5 hours), and growing automotive/battery cluster provides demand diversification. Poland's logistics market is forecast to see the strongest demand growth rates in Europe in 2026, with investment volumes projected to rise 19% (Savills). A dual-site strategy (warehouse in Dresden for JIT, finishing in Wrocław for cost optimization) merits evaluation.

## 4. Customer Industry Shift Analysis

### 4.1 Automotive (Largest Semiconductor Customer)

- **Trend:** OEMs moving volume production to CEE while keeping design in Western Europe.
  - Mercedes: eSprinter from Berlin → Jawor, Poland (€360M, production starting 2026, VAN.EA platform) [14]
  - BMW: Debrecen, Hungary — fully operational since October 2025, 50,000th iX3 by early 2026
  - Toyota: Circular Factory in Wałbrzych, Poland (February 2026)
- **Implication for semiconductor demand:** Design engineering stays near Dresden (Infineon, Bosch, ESMC customers); volume production increasingly in CEE. Semiconductor demand concentrates near fab locations because just-in-time delivery is critical for automotive qualification.

### 4.2 Industrial & Power Semiconductors

- Infineon's Smart Power Fab targets automotive, industrial, and renewable energy applications — the broadest demand base of any single fab.
- Power semiconductor demand driven by: EV adoption (onboard chargers, inverters), solar/wind inverters, industrial automation, data center power delivery.
- **Implication:** Diversified demand base reduces single-industry risk for the Dresden corridor.

### 4.3 AI / Data Center

- Growing but primarily US/Asia concentrated.
- EU sovereign compute push could create European demand, but timelines are uncertain.
- **Confidence:** Unconfirmed for European semiconductor manufacturing demand.

### 4.4 Defence

- EU defence spending: €418B in 2025 (+20% YoY), projected €454B in 2026 [9].
- Defence investment: €134B in 2025, projected €163B in 2026 (158.7% increase since 2021) [9].
- EDIP first calls launched for ammunition, missiles, and explosive weapons [11].
- Semiconductor demand: radar, electronic warfare, secure communications, guidance systems.
- **Confidence:** Derived — defence spending is confirmed but semiconductor-specific demand is not quantified.

### 4.5 Supply Chain Multiplier Effect

Industry analysis consistently shows each major semiconductor fab generates 3–5× in downstream supplier investment. Applied to the Dresden corridor:
- 4 fabs × average €4.3B investment = €17.1B direct
- × 3–5 multiplier = €51–85B total ecosystem potential by 2032
- Realistic projection (accounting for pipeline uncertainty): **€30–40B by 2032**

### 7.3 Key Market Themes (2026)

1. **Supply constraints dominate.** Planning, power grid capacity, and permitting are restricting new development more than demand weakness. Completions will decrease in 2026 as speculative pipelines remain controlled (CBRE via Erena).

4. **Manufacturing occupiers active in CEE.** Poland expected to see one of the strongest demand growth rates in Europe in 2026, driven by manufacturing and automotive logistics alongside e-commerce/3PL (Savills).
5. **Investment capital returning.** Core institutional investors re-entering after 2024–2025 hiatus. Prime yields stabilising. Cross-border capital dominant at 62% of volumes (Savills).

## 8. Warehouse / Finishing Facility Fit Analysis

### 8.1 What "Finishing" Means in Semiconductor Context

Semiconductor finishing encompasses post-fabrication operations:
- **Testing:** Wafer-level and packaged-device testing
- **Packaging:** Die attach, wire bonding, encapsulation, advanced packaging (flip-chip, 2.5D/3D)
- **Labeling & marking:** Product identification, traceability codes
- **Burn-in:** Accelerated life testing for reliability screening
- **Final QC:** Visual inspection, electrical characterization, shipping preparation

### 8.2 Proximity Requirements

- Semiconductor finishing must be within logistics range of fabs — ideally same-day truck delivery.
- Just-in-time delivery is critical for automotive qualification cycles.
- A facility >24 hours from the fab creates unacceptable lead time risk.

### 8.3 Dresden Corridor Fit

All four major fabs (ESMC, Infineon, GlobalFoundries, Bosch) are within **30km of each other** in the Dresden area. A finishing/warehouse facility in the corridor:
- Serves all four fabs with same-day logistics
- Benefits from Silicon Saxony's 130+ company ecosystem
- Access to established freight rail connections to Hamburg and Rotterdam ports
- Shares labor pool and infrastructure with the fab cluster

**Real estate market fit:** German logistics rents (€60–120/sqm/year) are at the higher end of European ranges, but the density of customer demand (4 fabs within 30km) justifies the premium. The market is stabilising — prime yields at ~4.5% suggest pricing has found its floor after the 2022–2024 correction.

### 8.4 Alternative: Wrocław Dual-Site

For cost optimization, a secondary finishing facility in Wrocław (PL51) offers:
- 40–50% lower industrial rents (€30–60/sqm/year vs. €60–120 in Germany)
- Rail connection to Dresden (2.5 hours on A4 corridor)
- Growing battery + automotive cluster for demand diversification
- EU single market access (Poland)
- Prime yields at 5.75–6.00% (vs. Germany's 4.5%) — higher yield reflects higher risk but also lower entry cost

**Recommended evaluation:** Dual-site strategy — warehouse in Dresden for JIT proximity, finishing operations in Wrocław for cost. The rent differential (potential €30–60/sqm/year savings) and 1.25–1.50% yield premium suggest meaningful cost advantage. Requires detailed logistics cost modeling to validate.

## 9. Recommendation

### 9.1 Primary Location: Saxony/Dresden (DE80)

**Rationale:**
- Proximity to €17B+ customer manufacturing base (4 major fabs within 30km)
- Same-day logistics to all major semiconductor customers
- Established ecosystem (Silicon Saxony: 130+ companies, 50,000+ jobs)
- Strong transport infrastructure (A4/A13/A17 motorways, rail, Dresden Airport)
- Deep technical workforce pipeline (TU Dresden, Fraunhofer)
- GDP per capita: €38,688 (2022) — mid-range for European logistics markets
- Logistics market stabilising: prime yields ~4.5%, rental growth moderating
- Germany logistics take-up +18% YTD — demand is real

**Risks to manage:**
- Labor market tightness from simultaneous mega-fab ramp-ups
- German regulatory complexity and permitting timelines
- Power grid congestion — engage early with grid operator (Savills notes this as a pan-European constraint)
- Higher cost base vs. Eastern Europe alternatives (€60–120/sqm vs. €30–60/sqm)

### 9.2 Alternative Location: Wrocław / Lower Silesia (PL51)

**Rationale:**
- 40–50% lower industrial rents and labor costs
- Rail-linked to Dresden (2.5 hours)
- GDP growth: €44.4B → €70.2B (2020–2024), +57.9% — faster growth than Dresden
- GDP per capita €22,800 (2022) — competitive cost base
- LG Energy Solution + Mercedes Jawor provide automotive/battery diversification
- EU single market access with lower cost structure
- Poland logistics: strongest demand growth forecast in Europe 2026 (Savills)
- Investment volumes forecast +19% in 2026 — capital is flowing in

**Risks to manage:**
- No semiconductor fabs — satellite location, not primary hub
- Polish regulatory environment less predictable than Germany
- Infrastructure quality variable outside Wrocław metro
- Power grid congestion likely (Pan-European issue per Savills)

### 9.3 Recommended Strategy

**Dual-site evaluation recommended:**
1. **Primary warehouse (Dresden):** JIT proximity to all four fabs. Accept higher cost for customer proximity. €60–120/sqm/year justified by density of demand.
2. **Finishing operations (Wrocław):** Cost-optimized finishing, testing, packaging. Rail-linked for efficient material flow. €30–60/sqm/year with 5.75–6.00% prime yields.
3. **Trigger for dual-site:** If finishing labor cost differential exceeds €2M/year and logistics cost is manageable (<€0.50/unit incremental).
4. **Power grid:** Engage early with grid operator in both locations — this is now the #1 constraint on new logistics development across Europe per Savills/CBRE.
